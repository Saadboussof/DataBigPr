1.  Context & Problem Statement 
 
 
Casablanca is the economic capital of Morocco and home to over 4 million inhabitants. Despite 
significant investment in formal transit infrastructure — the BRT corridor along Boulevard 
Mohammed VI, the ONCF suburban rail, and thousands of licensed taxis — urban mobility remains 
deeply fragmented. The core problem is not a shortage of vehicles: it is the total absence of a data 
layer connecting supply to demand. 
 
Pain Point 
Reality in Casablanca 
No shared data layer 
Grand taxis, petits taxis, and informal minibuses operate with no 
GPS tracking, no digital booking, no shared schedule 
Demand blindness 
Drivers cruise looking for passengers; passengers wait with no 
visibility of vehicle availability 
No interoperability 
Formal transit (ONCF, BRT) and informal taxis share no data, no 
ticketing, no trip planner 
Cash-only payments 
Zero payment data means zero trip history, zero analytics, zero 
personalization 
Underserved periphery 
New districts (Bouskoura, Ain Sebaâ, Sidi Moumen) grow faster 
than routes are planned 
 
TaaSim is a Transport-as-a-Service platform that treats urban mobility as a data engineering 
problem. By ingesting GPS vehicle streams, processing citizen trip reservations in real time, and 
applying batch analytics and machine learning to historical patterns, TaaSim can match riders to 
vehicles dynamically, forecast demand surges, and give city planners a unified analytical view of 
the mobility network.
